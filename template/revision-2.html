Approach

A language model as the analyst. A machine as the trader.

Why we put Claude at the center of a quantitative system, and how the model and the code divide the work.

Why a language model

A good trade rests on evidence that arrives in many forms at once: numbers, charts, and the behaviour of other traders. Fixed rules handle the numbers and miss the context. A person reads the context but tires, hesitates and drifts from the plan. Claude reads all of it on every decision, applies the same rulebook each time, and writes down its reasoning.

The quantitative base

Everything Claude sees is computed first, in tested code. We collect data from four exchanges and compute more than twenty indicators in-house, covering trend, momentum, volume and volatility. The key indicators update live as each candle forms and are checked against TradingView.

Why low frequency suits Claude

We trade a small number of high-conviction setups, and a setup can take hours or days to fill. At that pace a few seconds of model thinking costs nothing, so every decision gets the strongest Claude model and the full picture. The work that needs speed, watching price every second and sending orders, stays in code.

The decision loop

How one market event becomes a trade

  1. 01

    Watch

    Code

    Fifteen detectors scan the data around the clock. Claude stays idle until one of them flags something worth a closer look, such as a break in market structure, a new 4-hour or daily candle, or price reaching a key level.

  2. 02

    Read

    Claude

    Claude pulls the history and live readings it needs through tools, and reads the charts and market maps it asks for.

  3. 03

    Plan

    Claude

    It writes a board. Each setup gets an entry band, a stop, targets, its odds of reaching the first target, and the exact conditions under which it may fire.

  4. 04

    Hold

    Code

    A watcher checks those conditions every second, with no model in the loop. When an entry condition is met inside the band, fixed code sizes the position and places the order.

  5. 05

    Manage

    Both

    In a trade, Claude sets warning and exit conditions. A warning wakes it to reassess. An exit condition closes the trade without waiting for the model.

  6. 06

    Review

    Claude

    When the trade closes, Claude writes the journal entry and the desk returns to idle, ready for the next setup.

Risk controls

Limits the model cannot override

  • ExposureOne open position per account at any time.
  • Margin & leverageIsolated margin only, with a hard 20x leverage cap.
  • Stop-lossEvery order carries a predefined stop-loss. No stop, no order.
  • Entry bandSignals that trigger outside the planned entry range are rejected automatically. No chasing.
  • Position sizingSize is set by deterministic, reviewed code. The model’s only input is its probability estimate.
  • FailsafesStop-losses rest on the exchange and execute without waiting on the model. API keys have withdrawals disabled.

Claude across the company

From the first design session to the trade journal

Claude has been part of every stage of the work since the beginning, from research to production.

Research
The trading method was refined on the Anantaka desk with Claude as the analyst, and the architecture was worked out in long design sessions with Claude.
Engineering
The system is built with Claude Code. Features start as written design specs, and the repository carries its own working instructions for Claude.
Decisions
In production Claude is the analyst. It runs through the Claude Agent SDK with tools, chart reading, and one continuous conversation for each trade.
Review
We audit the system with Claude. The September review checked 22 findings against the code and set the order of fixes.
Learning
Claude writes a journal entry for every trade, and the desk reviews what worked and what did not before the next one.

Timeline

From a trading desk to a trading machine

Years of trading came first. The machine has been in engineering since May 2026.

  1. 2020

    Futuh starts trading crypto markets

    The trading experience that Valence is built on begins.

  2. Jul 2024

    Anantaka starts trading

    Anantaka, a sister desk in Halven Group, trades BTC perpetual futures with a written, rule-based framework. Claude later becomes the desk’s analyst.

  3. Apr 2026

    Work begins on automating the desk

    We compare three frontier AI models on the same desk data and start specifying the system.

  4. 5 May 2026

    Engineering starts

    The three founders split the work across quant and product, data engineering, and AI engineering. The database schema and the first indicator pipelines ship.

  5. 27 Jun 2026

    We choose Claude

    Claude reasoned the most systematically of the models we tested, so we dropped the multi-model design and built on Claude alone. The trade lifecycle is fixed: idle, setup, in trade, review.

  6. Jul 2026

    The data engine is complete

    Indicators from four exchanges are checked bar by bar against TradingView. The automation is designed around the Claude Agent SDK.

  7. 11 Aug 2026

    The system becomes Valence

    Claude now writes the conditions for each trade in a form the machine can check, and a watcher holds them every second.

  8. Sep 2026

    Orders run on testnet

    Execution runs through a simulator and Binance testnet. On 30 September a full system review checks 22 findings against the code, with 1,591 tests passing.

  9. Oct 2026

    Entry and risk, rebuilt Now

    Entry and risk logic are redesigned, and the review’s fixes land one by one.

  10. Next

    Live trading

    Valence goes live once the pre-live checklist from the review is cleared.
